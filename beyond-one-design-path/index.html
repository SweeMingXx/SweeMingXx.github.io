🧭 The Macro Horizon: Design 2035 Master Plan

Kelly Tan detailed Singapore's national master plan, articulating that design is not mere stylistic embellishment, but a national engine for economic competitiveness and civic well-being.

“By 2035, every Singaporean will use design at work or in life, shaping Singapore to be a livable, lovable, and business-driven nation by design.” — Kelly Tan, DesignSingapore Council

The In-House Reality: Why Business Acumen Is Non-Negotiable

Nationwide data demonstrates that of the 61,000 full-time design personnel, 35,000 operate inside non-design sectors (banking, healthcare, government, logistics) compared to only 11,000 in dedicated design agencies. In other words, 76% of Singapore's designers work in-house.

In-house designers cannot afford to stay inside a visual echo chamber. Designers must possess transdisciplinary fluency and business acumen: articulating value not in terms of color theory, but in bottom-line operational ROI: "This intervention reduced workflow latency by X% and saved $Y in user drop-off".

🤖 The Designer's Role in the Age of AI

With foundation models like Claude, ChatGPT, and DeepSeek paired with agentic developer environments like Cursor, how do human designers preserve an outsized edge?

🔥
Hunger to Learn Trumps 20 Years of Tenure

Generative AI workflows have only matured over the last 18 to 24 months. A design director with 25 years of legacy experience is encountering agentic tools at the exact same moment as a novice. An insatiable hunger to tinker, fail, and build personal tools levels the field faster than static years on a resume.

🧠
Design Judgment & Sense-Making

AI generates infinite options; humans provide discernment. Design judgment is the deliberate ability to rationalize subjective aesthetics into objective markers that align with business constraints and build stakeholder trust. Current LLMs cannot independently triangulate unstructured corporate politics, legacy code constraints, and raw human emotion.

🔄
The Convergence: Rise of the "Feature Owner"

The rigid wall separating Product Managers (PRDs), UI Designers (Figma), and Software Developers (Code) is dissolving. Future designers will operate as complete feature owners: framing problems, conducting user discovery, architecting UI, and deploying frontend builds directly.

💬
Dancing with the Tool, Not Vending Machines

Treating LLMs like transactional vending machines yields generic results. High-performing builders "dance" with the model: using evocative natural language (e.g., "make this transition feel like a waterfall"), feeding screenshots, and iterating dynamically without needing traditional syntax mastery.

⚡ Critical Industry Tensions & Strategic Debates

Modern design practitioners navigate four recurring tensions highlighted during the panel discussion:

User Advocacy vs. Business Realities

Balancing pure human-centered empathy with strict corporate KPIs, development budgets, and leadership metrics needed to greenlight features.

Rapid Shipping vs. Craft Perfection

Deciding when to freeze building to deploy an imperfect v1 MVP onto TestFlight versus endlessly polishing micro-interactions in isolation.

Convenience Culture vs. Lovable Friction

Weighing seamless hyper-optimization against intentional, delightful friction that builds memorable emotional resonance and deeper learning.

Craft Specialization vs. Role Convergence

Choosing between remaining a specialized craftsperson versus adapting into a hybrid generalist spanning user research, product strategy, and frontend code.

🛠️ Practical Field Playbook: Lessons from Non-Traditional Builders

1. Never "Pretend-Play the User"

Owen shared that his most costly early-career mistake was assuming his personal intuition matched user reality. Designers must anchor interface architectures in observable behavioral markers and contextual inquiry.

2. Empathize with Internal Teammates as Personas

Fiki applied his anthropology background by treating engineers, PMs, and executives as user personas. Understand their specific sprint constraints, technical debt, and KPIs. Stakeholder management is UX research turned inward.

3. Physical-to-Digital Onboarding Metaphors

Arriving at an unfamiliar physical space without guidance creates immediate hesitation. Digital applications require the same hospitable onboarding warmth—guiding the user smoothly to remove uncertainty before revealing complex features.

4. Validate Distribution Before Writing Code

Fiki reversed traditional software development cycles:

  1. Find the Habitat: Observe where target users congregate (e.g., tracking viral music trends on TikTok and Instagram).
  2. Prototype Single Screens: Create one core screen or standalone iOS widget.
  3. Measure Public Demand: Post screen recordings to evaluate waitlist sign-ups before coding backends or databases.

Willingness to pay or recurring retention remains the only ultimate business validation.

5. TestFlight Analytics Over Staged Testing

Deploy functional MVPs directly to TestFlight. In Fiki's music app, telemetry revealed users ignoring embedded YouTube players and repeatedly tapping individual lyric lines to jump audio—unveiling true emergent behaviors that guided the roadmap.

6. Suppress Ego & Prune Ruthlessly

AI makes feature generation cheap, creating dangerous software bloat. Build v1 products with local device storage, zero backends, no login walls, and 1–3 core features. If data shows users do not care, kill the feature immediately without pride.

7. Joyful Friction Over Sterile Convenience

In an era where software races toward soulless efficiency, intentional friction creates emotional connection. Fiki's calendar app features a deliberate, satisfying animation of a physical page tearing away. In education, struggle is inherent to learning; design's duty is making that friction delightful.

💬 Audience Q&A Deep Dive

Teardown of the four key questions raised by the audience during the interactive Q&A session:

Audience Question: How can indie creators validate whether an app will gain genuine traction before committing extensive engineering hours?

Fiki's Answer: Always begin with distribution discovery. Observe where users already vocalize unmet needs (e.g., TikTok videos of students transcribing song lyrics to learn Chinese). Build a single mockup screen or iOS widget, publish video recordings online, and measure interest through organic waitlist sign-ups before touching backend code.

Aznan's Question (SP UXC): If design research is bucket one and cross-disciplinary stakeholder management is bucket two, what is the crucial third component designers need today?

Fiki's Answer: Suppression of Ego. Never get married to your design solution. If analytics reveal users do not care about a feature, kill it swiftly without personal attachment.

Owen's Answer: Role Agility & Convergence. Stop clinging rigidly to the title of "designer". Expand into product strategy, project leadership, and software engineering to survive as AI blurs operational boundaries.

Chichi's Question: How do builders break past perfectionism and identify the hard boundary where you must stop building and ship?

Owen's Answer: Hitting your AI token limits serves as a humorous but highly practical boundary!

Fiki's Answer: Set strict architectural constraints on version 1.0: zero backend databases, local-only storage, no login barriers, and no more than 1–3 core features. Ship early directly to TestFlight and let real telemetry data dictate subsequent feature development.

Ray's Question: In an industry obsessed with frictionless convenience, can intentional friction make a product more lovable and engaging?

Fiki's Answer: Absolutely. In education, personal development, and craft, friction is the essential crucible of learning. Designers must invent "joyful friction"—such as tactile page-peel animations or learning Mandarin via hip-hop rhythms—to make essential struggle deeply rewarding and charming.
