02 BEYOND THE DEMO

Intelligence isn’t one straight line.

A model can perform strongly in some settings yet fail on something that seems simple. Three lenses help make sense of the gap between a great answer and a trustworthy one.

01

Capability

Can the system do the task at all? An impressive result in one domain does not establish all-round understanding.

02

Reliability

Can it do the task consistently enough to be trusted? One good response is not the same as dependable performance.

03

Calibration

Does its confidence match its accuracy? The TV example is unsettling precisely because the wrong answer sounded plausible.

THE RISK EQUATIONIncorrect reasoning+persuasive explanation+human over-trust→poor decisions

05 PUT IT TO WORK

Prepare for uncertainty, not a single date.

These are practical interpretations of the talk’s framing, rather than direct instructions from Stillwell. They remain useful across several possible AI futures.

Verify what matters

Build human review into decisions where a plausible error would have real consequences.

Track capability & reliability

Ask not only what AI can do, but how consistently it can do it in your actual context.

Strengthen governance

Invest in clear safeguards, oversight and accountability for increasingly capable systems.

Design for people

Make uncertainty visible in human–AI experiences instead of disguising it with polished output.

Plan multiple futures

Use scenarios to guide skills, education and organisational choices without betting on one forecast.

Keep human judgement

Value the ability to spot when a confident answer still needs to be challenged.

The editorial bottom line

Don’t mistake today’s weaknesses for permanent limits. Don’t mistake polished output for all-round intelligence.

AI is both astonishing and imperfect. Its trajectory remains uncertain — and consequential enough to deserve thoughtful preparation now.
