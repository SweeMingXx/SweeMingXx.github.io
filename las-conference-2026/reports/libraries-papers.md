## Executive Summary & Session Overview
The afternoon concurrent paper track at the Theatre brought together groundbreaking practical implementations from Singapore Management University (SMU) and Singapore Polytechnic (SP). ## Paper 1: Libraries as Enablers of Trusted Research Data Infrastructure
Dong Danping (co-authored with Janna Xu Cong) outlined SMU Libraries' strategic role in the university-wide Data Stacks initiative led by the Vice Provost for Strategic Research Partnerships. While conventional Research Data Management (RDM) emphasizes publishing open, de-identified datasets, impactful modern social science requires analyzing highly sensitive, confidential administrative datasets (e.g., longitudinal records from the Singapore Life Panel covering 10,000 elderly citizens).

To enable cross-agency research without privacy leakage, SMU integrated GovTech's secure Trusted Research Environment (inTrust/TRUST). While SMU IT manages platform server architecture and Amazon Workspaces virtualization, the Library was officially funded and appointed as the researcher-facing operations arm. The Library provides metadata discovery, researcher onboarding, regulatory guidance, and data anonymization training using GovTech's CLOAK cryptographic hashing tool.

### SMU Data Stacks: secure research data architecture
- 1. inTrust Catalog — Library Metadata Discovery
- 2. CLOAK Tool — Key Hashing & Pseudonymization
- 3. Secure Sandbox — Virtual Workspaces (No Raw Export)
- Audited Results — Admin-Approved Output

### Bridging Information Literacy to Regulatory Compliance
By mediating the data ingestion pathway, SMU Librarians resolve the chronic problem where researchers struggle to establish complex data-sharing agreements with public ministries (e.g., MOH, MOE, HDB). Researchers analyze pseudonymized records within sandboxed Amazon Workspaces without downloading raw data.

Dong emphasized that data librarianship must expand beyond open repositories toward high-value research facilitation: "Researchers don't just want to make their data open; they ask, 'What valuable data can you get for me?'" Operating as inTrust navigators positions librarians at the core of national strategic research agendas.

## Paper 2: Embedding AI-Enabled Databases into Engineering Curricula
Wan Ho Wah (SP Library) and Bo Lan (School of Electrical & Electronic Engineering) presented the curricular integration of the Mintel Global New Products Database and its generative AI concept tool, Spark. Embedded across 15 classes totaling 278 Year 2 students in core module ET1010 (Microcontroller Applications), the platform supports the CDIO (Conceive-Design-Implement-Operate) framework, which constitutes 75% of module assessment.

Students integrate Singapore Green Plan 2030 and UN SDGs into product design. Rather than relying on unguided ChatGPT prompts, students utilize Mintel's structured industry prompts to generate consumer personas, market viability assessments, and visual prototypes, which teams critically interrogate using 2x2 decision matrices.

## Paper 3: Academic Library as Strategic Partner in Redesign
Dr. Kwa Lay Ping (School of Life Skills & Communication) and Wong Cheng Long (SP Library) demonstrated how libraries actively resolve faculty blind spots during macro curriculum overhauls. In response to industry demands for graduates with advanced critical thinking and workplace communication, SP launched comprehensive module reviews.

### The Four-Stage Strategic Consultation Model
Moving past passive 40-page bibliography packets, SP Library established a structured 4-step framework: Scope (defining faculty priorities early), Relevance Sensing (synthesizing industry whitepapers into concise 3-2-1 briefs), Comparative Evidence (benchmarking peer polytechnic models), and Takeover (co-designing pedagogical case studies).

Wong noted that academic librarians add the highest value through evidence synthesis. The model is expanding across all nine academic schools within Singapore Polytechnic, establishing a permanent service pipeline for triennial institutional course reviews.

## Strategic Directives for Academic & Research Libraries
- Position Libraries Within Secure Data Environments: Expand data services beyond open data advocacy to operationalize national trusted research environments (TREs), training researchers in data anonymization, CLOAK hashing, and secure cloud navigation.
- Embed Specialized AI Tools Directly into Applied Modules: Transition library e-resource subscriptions from passive access links into structured pedagogical assets embedded in multidisciplinary project workflows (e.g., CDIO).
- Replace Lengthy Reading Lists with Actionable Intelligence: Adopt structured synthesis models (such as SP's 3-2-1 briefs) to deliver concise environmental scanning and cross-institutional benchmarking directly to curriculum taskforces.
- Institutionalize Joint Faculty-Library Governance: Establish formalized consultative service pathways for institutional course reviews, pairing faculty disciplinary knowledge with library information architecture and horizon scanning.
