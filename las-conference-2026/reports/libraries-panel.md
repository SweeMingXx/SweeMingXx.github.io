Ecosystems of Respite, Public Luxuries, and Microclimates for Deep Reading in an Automated Age

## Executive Summary & Audience Pulse
Following the opening keynote, Theme 1 brought together an interdisciplinary panel uniting educational research, independent publishing, urban ecological planning, and academic librarianship. Sponsored by IEEE, the session interrogated how libraries must evolve as generative AI and attention fragmentation reshape human habits.

An interactive audience poll of over 200 library delegates revealed two acute anxieties dominating the profession: declining deep reading and critical thinking, alongside the substitution of primary source engagement with synthetic AI answers. In response, the panel formulated a radical defense of libraries not merely as digital transaction portals, but as "public luxuries" and countercultural sanctuaries. The discussion synthesized three core paradigms: viewing reading as respite, engineering physical and systemic "microclimates" for slow inquiry, and designing psychological and environmental community shelters.

### 1. Reading as Respite
Moving beyond purely transactional literacy to position print reading as a conscious refuge from digital surveillance and device fatigue.

### 2. Slow-Down Microclimates
Creating physical and institutional spaces that intentionally slow pace down, counteracting relentless capitalization and algorithmic speed.

### 3. Public Luxuries
Upholding shared civic assets and psychological shelter as a direct democratic counterweight to rising economic and social precarity.

## Adolescent Literacy & the Ecology of Respite (A/P Loh Chin Ee)
Drawing from intensive fieldwork tracking adolescent reading across classrooms and domestic settings, Assoc. Prof. Loh Chin Ee debunked the myth that digital-native teenagers exclusively favor digital screens. For long-form fiction, non-fiction, and narrative comics, adolescents consistently prefer print books, turning online primarily for news or specialized manga when physical copies are checked out.

Loh advocated for a developmental print-and-digital continuum: print books serve as unifunctional artifacts that protect developing brains from sensory splintering, while digital platforms cater to adult multi-sourcing. Crucially, contemporary youth and adult book clubs increasingly frame reading as respite—an intentional sanctuary to escape smartphone ubiquity. In an era where boredom instantly triggers algorithmic scrolling, cultivating deep reading demands deliberate boundaries and structured time.

- Public Luxury — Shared Civic Wealth

## Capitalization, Digitalization & Slow-Down Industries (Ng Kah Gay)
Ethos Books publisher Ng Kah Gay examined the systemic pressures exerted by capitalization—the economic imperative where public services must quantify return on investment (ROI). In nations like the UK (where over 780 public libraries closed over a decade), ROI metrics gutted civic infrastructure. While Singapore continues robust civic funding, libraries increasingly justify budgets through digitalization grants tied to AI inclusion initiatives.

Ng warned that if digitalization merely accelerates automated content generation, it risks accelerating the demise of contemplation. He characterized publishing and librarianship as "slow-down industries" whose democratic mandate is to construct friction against algorithmic consumerism. By curating countercultural literature and hosting physical encounters, libraries introduce a necessary "braking effect" that compels patrons to pause, question, and reflect.

## Public Luxuries & Civic Resilience (Sarah Ichioka)
Urban futurist Sarah Ichioka challenged delegates to champion libraries as public luxuries—drawing on environmental writer George Monbiot's thesis that flourishing societies pair private sufficiency with opulent, shared civic commons to curb inequality. Amid intensifying climate and social shocks, libraries serve as frontline resilience infrastructure: offering physical refuge during extreme heat and haze crises, and psychological shelter during economic disruption.

Ichioka linked this role to technological displacement, where generative AI threatens livelihoods from transport operators to financial analysts. Comparing this disruption to her great-grandfather finding daily solace and job listings at the Chicago Public Library during the Great Depression, she affirmed that the library's combined hardware (shelter, desks) and software (communal dignity, solidarity) builds collective human agency.

## Cognitive Friction: Discernment vs. Sycophancy
The panel examined the neurological difference between interactive human dialogue and synthetic AI responses. Loh shared a research vignette illustrating the temptation of AI discovery engines (e.g., Consensus): when revising research late at night, scholars face the acute temptation to cite automated summaries without opening original texts, underscoring that critical reading is an ethical discipline.

### The Sycophancy Dilemma & Active Knowledge Production
Sarah Ichioka noted that commercial AI models are engineered to be sycophantic to maintain user engagement. To resist cognitive passivity, researchers must prompt AI for pushback rather than answers—treating reading and writing as an interconnected, shared coral reef that requires collective restoration rather than automated extraction.

Loh noted that while conversations with visiting scholar Prof. Naomi Baron consistently provoked critical thinking, AI models default to immediate answers. Sustained intellectual growth requires engaging works that challenge assumptions rather than flattering them.

## Key Takeaways & Core Strategic Insights
### Executive takeaways
- Reading as Respite: Adolescents and adults actively turn to print to escape screen fatigue and algorithmically forced choices.
- The Coexistence Model: Move beyond print vs. digital binaries; print anchors deep learning, while digital enables broad access.
- Slow-Down Industries: Independent publishing and libraries serve as essential brakes against frantic consumerism and trend-chasing.
- Public Luxury Defense: Libraries provide physical and psychological shelter, shielding communities from inequality and tech dislocation.

### Core strategic insights
- Creating Microclimates: When macro-policy lags, libraries can establish local physical and social environments where contemplation thrives.
- The Sycophancy Problem: AI tools are engineered to flatter users; real discernment requires engaging materials that introduce friction and challenge.
- Restoring the Creative Reef: Reconnecting readers and writers closes the knowledge loop, turning consumers into active producers of meaning.
- Step Out of Invisibility: Librarians must make their behind-the-scenes algorithmic sorting, curation, and guidance visible to stakeholders.
