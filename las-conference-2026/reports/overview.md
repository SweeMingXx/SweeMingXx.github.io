## Executive Summary & Overarching Mandate
Held at the University Cultural Centre, the 2026 Library Association of Singapore (LAS) Conference convened more than 400 delegates representing national, public, academic, polytechnic, school, and special libraries. Doubling attendance from previous editions, the two-day gathering tackled the systemic disruptions wrought by generative artificial intelligence, algorithmic attention fragmentation, and shifting epistemic paradigms.

Across two opening keynotes, three high-level plenary panels, twelve concurrent research papers, and fourteen lightning poster presentations, the conference forged an unambiguous consensus: libraries must reject passive technological determinism. Rather than serving as mere "humans in the loop" who react to machine automation, information professionals must assert themselves as "Humans in the Lead"—guiding critical discernment, defending deep reading, and anchoring democratic knowledge equity.

## Theme 1: Future of Libraries — Public Luxuries & the Bi-Literate Brain
Day 1 opened with an impassioned defense of human agency. LAS President Shameem Nilofar critiqued linguistic habits that make AI the active grammatical subject (AI transforms, AI disrupts), reminding delegates that adapting is an active human verb requiring collective solidarity. In his Opening Keynote, NLB Deputy Chair Warren Fernandez addressed the attention crisis and declining global PISA reading comprehension scores, invoking Harold Bloom: "Information is endlessly available to us, but where shall wisdom be found?"

Fernandez articulated three timeless pillars: cultivating sustained readers through national movements (Read SG), serving as critical guides who deconstruct AI citations to their roots, and curating living collective memory. In the subsequent plenary panel, educational researcher Assoc. Prof. Loh Chin Ee established that teenagers still favor physical print for deep immersion, turning to reading as respite from smartphones. Publisher Ng Kah Gay challenged ROI-driven capitalization, framing libraries as "slow-down industries" that create microclimates for reflection, while urban futurist Sarah Ichioka championed libraries as essential "public luxuries" offering climate and economic resilience.

### Communities, Not Commodities: Language Diversity (Setsuko Yokoyama)
In her Day 1 Keynote Lecture, SUTD literary historian Asst. Prof. Setsuko Yokoyama delivered a sweeping thirty-year genealogy linking digital humanities and librarianship. Deconstructing generative AI, she revealed how speech-to-text tools uncritically mirror 1940s corporate secretarial dictation, devaluing transcription into underpaid "ghost work."

Yokoyama championed New Zealand's Te Hiku Media—which achieved a 92% Māori speech recognition accuracy by training on 30 years of indigenous oral recordings—as an inspiring model for Singapore to preserve its own rich heritage languages (Hokkien, Teochew, Cantonese, Baba Malay). She urged libraries to demystify AI parameters for young learners, transforming black-box algorithms into transparent objects of critical source study.

## Theme 2: Future of Technology — Workflows, Models & Living Memory
Day 2 opened with Theme 2, examining the concrete operationalisation of artificial intelligence across institutions. Singapore Computer Society President Lim Bee Kwan analyzed the changing architecture of employment: AI takes away routine tasks (high compressibility) rather than entire professions, but creates an urgent societal dilemma—the vanishing bottom rungs of the career ladder for fresh graduates.

AI Singapore's Dr. Ngui Jian Gang presented the open-source SEA-LION model, noting that cultural representation in Southeast Asia must be built collaboratively with regional native speakers rather than centralized under state monopolies. Addressing the archival frontier, Dr. Natalia Grincheva (LASALLE/UAS) highlighted the "flat-file blind spot" of legacy repositories, demonstrating how community-authored VR folklore and executable digital art lack institutional archiving platforms, demanding containerized, executable preservation.

### Frontline Applied Implementations: From Data Stacks to LibBee
Across concurrent sessions, Singapore institutions showcased advanced technical implementations. SMU Libraries operationalized GovTech's inTrust/CLOAK secure data infrastructure, enabling social scientists to query confidential government records without privacy leakage. Concurrently, NLB unveiled its Entity Data Service (EDS), converting 3 million cultural records from isolated strings into linked semantic entities across English and Chinese scripts.

### Applied Technical Agency: In-House AI Engineering
Rejecting costly vendor black boxes, Ngee Ann Polytechnic engineered LibBee AI in-house using dual-layer RAG and live APIs (Alma, Primo, LibCal) to power an unmanned library with Socratic assignment guidance. Similarly, Singapore Polytechnic embedded Mintel Spark AI into engineering capstones, demonstrating how libraries guide applied learning.

Frontline staff also led automation breakthroughs: Jessie Tan (NIE) deployed local open Gemma models to parse restricted exam papers without writing software, winning First Prize at the poster awards.

## Theme 3: Future of Librarianship — The Competency Paradigm Shift
Theme 3 addressed the professional identity, qualifications, and future roles of information workers. Vincent Wong (NTU) demonstrated how academic librarians successfully repositioned themselves as institutional transformation leaders, delivering AI upskilling micro-projects to university administrators. Makeswary Periasamy (NLB) chronicled the evolution of rare collections from medieval chained books and locked vaults to public learning laboratories, donor co-stewardship, and youth engagement.

Presenting an empirical study of 358 Singapore library job postings (2021–2026), Janna Xu Cong (SMU) revealed that 82% of AI job openings demand "AI user" skills rather than computer science coding, while interpersonal communication and stakeholder management appear in over 75% of advertisements. NTU University Librarian Caroline Pang affirmed this hiring reality: academic libraries seek candidates with learning agility, empathy, and collaborative spirit, noting that technical tools can be taught on the job.

### The Capstone Panel: Managing the AI Intern
The closing panel, moderated by Alex Foo (MDDI), tackled the future of professional accreditation. An informal room poll saw zero delegates affirming the MLIS as an essential prerequisite to enter the field. Gee Miaw Miin (NLB/LAS Council) announced the restructuring of LAS's Professional Development Scheme (PDS) toward competency portfolios, alongside NLB's dual-track career ladders enabling specialist librarians to achieve senior principal director status without entering managerial administration.

Aaron Tay (SMU) urged librarians to understand agentic harnesses and semantic search to critically interrogate vendor platforms, noting that generative coding tools now allow non-programmers to close the execution gap. Gee offered the defining operational metaphor of the session: librarians must treat AI like an intern—delegating routine tasks while rigorously inspecting outputs, rather than deferring to algorithms as an unassailable boss.

## Strategic Directives & Road to CONSAL 2028
### Executive takeaways
- Human in the Lead: Reclaim professional agency over technology; librarians must actively direct and verify AI tools.
- Competency Over Paper: The sector has pivoted to portfolio-based recognition (LAS PDS) and dual specialist advancement tracks.
- Grounded Verification: Library collections provide the verified grounding required to cure commercial LLM hallucinations.
- Public Luxury Mandate: Libraries endure by bridging societal inequality, offering psychological and physical community shelter.

### Core strategic insights
- The Living Organism: Ranganathan's 5th Law requires libraries to evolve from static print repositories to executable code discovery.
- Closing the Execution Gap: Generative coding empowers non-technical staff to engineer bespoke in-house discovery solutions.
- Bridging Haves and Have-Nots: In an era of information excess, the library's scarcity value lies in cultivating critical discernment.
- CONSAL 2028 Milestone: Singapore will host 1,000+ regional leaders, celebrating library excellence across Southeast Asia.
