## Executive Summary & Overview
The afternoon concurrent session at the Theatre addressed the core evolution of the profession itself under Theme 3: Future of Librarianship. Rather than viewing professional roles through theoretical speculation, the three papers provided grounded empirical evidence and institutional case studies examining how librarians are proactively expanding their operational scope, transforming custodial legacies into public labs, and navigating real-world labor market demands.

The presentations established an interconnected professional arc: NTU Libraries demonstrated how librarians transitioned into campus-wide AI facilitators for administrative staff via hands-on "micro-projects" and clinic sessions; The National Library Board chronicled the structural paradigm shift in rare collections from inward-looking vault custodianship to outward-looking public education, co-stewardship, and digital advocacy; and SMU Libraries presented a rigorous 5-year empirical analysis of 358 Singapore library job postings, illustrating how AI skills are diffusing across non-technical library roles while core interpersonal communication remains paramount.

### 1. Micro-Project Upskilling
NTU trains university administrators in applied AI through bite-sized, 2-hour daily workflow automations and drop-in clinics.

### 2. Custodian to Collaborator
NLB transforms rare collections from locked archival vaults into interactive learning laboratories and donor co-stewardship.

### 3. Empirical Hiring Landscape
SMU examines 358 job ads, showing 82% of AI mentions focus on practical user adoption while human soft skills dominate.

## NTU: Beyond Traditional AI Literacy (Vincent Wong)
Vincent Wong (co-authored with Chia Yew Boon) detailed NTU Libraries' innovative approach to building institutional capability. With NTU mandating that 40% of all university modules become AI-enabled by 2030 alongside a compulsory undergraduate AI literacy curriculum, campus anxiety escalated. NTU Libraries recognized an opportunity to expand beyond their traditional student/faculty audience by delivering targeted AI literacy to university administrative colleagues across Student Affairs, Admissions, and Career Offices.

The program anchored technical instruction in a growth mindset workshop to alleviate change fatigue. Participants executed scoped "micro-projects"—practical workflow automations completed within two hours, such as using generative tools to parse complex vendor bills into structured spreadsheets. Supported by weekly on-site office clinic sessions, brown-bag lunch discussions, and digital badging via AI Singapore, the initiative trained over 80 staff, establishing the library as a campus-wide transformation catalyst.

### NTU Libraries: institutional AI upskilling architecture
- 1. Mindset & Ethics — Growth Mindset & AI Safety
- 2. Hands-on Micro-Projects — 2-Hour Daily Workflow Tasks
- 3. Embedded Support — Office Clinics & Champions
- Formal Recognition — HR Appraisals & AISG Badges

## NLB: Reimagining Rare Collections (Makeswary Periasamy)
Makeswary Periasamy traced the profound generational transformation of rare books librarianship at NLB. Tracing the shift from chained medieval books and rigid physical access controls to modern digital engagement, she explained how rare collections have transitioned from an inward-looking custodial focus (preserving fragile paper in vaults) into vibrant outward-facing learning laboratories.

NLB balances preservation with active democratic access—inviting students into reading rooms to view primary source manuscripts, developing co-stewardship acquisition models with external donors, and conducting ethical provenance scrutiny to ensure acquired artifacts were not illicitly trafficked. Rare librarians now act as content creators, digital humanities partners, and public advocates engaging youth in unearthing decentralized historical narratives.

## SMU: Empirical Post-LLM Job Market Analysis (Janna Xu Cong)
Janna Xu Cong presented a rigorous empirical study analyzing 358 unique Singapore library job advertisements posted between 2021 and August 2026. Rejecting speculative hype, Xu sought to establish an empirical baseline of how employers are actually modifying job descriptions following the emergence of Large Language Models.

The dataset revealed that 51 postings explicitly contained AI-specific vocabularies. Corroborating a 2025 PwC Singapore labor report indicating that 82% of AI job openings demand "AI user" capabilities rather than raw model development, Xu demonstrated that AI competencies have diffused beyond IT units into reference, instruction, collections, policy, and scholarly communications. Crucially, academic libraries contributed the highest volume of AI-related postings, predominantly targeted at Senior Librarian and Team Lead tiers.

## The Employer Perspective: Hiring the Quintessential Librarian
During the extended plenary discussion, NTU University Librarian Caroline Pang offered critical commentary from the hiring side of the table. Addressing the absence of a direct undergraduate library pipeline in Singapore, Pang clarified what academic library directors prioritize when reviewing candidate profiles.

### Caroline Pang (NTU University Librarian) on Hiring Realities
Pang emphasized that while basic conceptual familiarity with LLMs and prompt engineering is assumed, technical AI skills can be taught on the job. Academic libraries seek candidates exhibiting high learning agility, collaborative empathy, stakeholder diplomacy, and extroverted communication skills capable of engaging diverse university communities.

Xu's findings strongly validated this leadership perspective: over 255 job descriptions explicitly prioritized human communication, stakeholder negotiation, and instructional design, demonstrating that technological fluency complements rather than displaces foundational professional virtues.

## Key Takeaways & Core Strategic Insights
### Executive takeaways
- Micro-Project Scaffolding: Breaking automation into 2-hour daily tasks eliminates workplace resistance and builds genuine confidence.
- Beyond Vault Gatekeeping: Rare collections thrive as interactive pedagogical spaces, donor partnerships, and community archives.
- Pervasive AI User Role: AI expectations have diffused into 82% user-level applications across every major library operational unit.
- The Primacy of Human Soft Skills: Communication, empathy, and teamwork appear in over 75% of job ads, outweighing pure tech coding.

### Core strategic insights
- Campus-Wide Relevance: By upskilling non-library administrative staff, academic libraries position themselves at institutional centers.
- Ethical Provenance Stewardship: Modern heritage curation requires deep legal scrutiny, decolonization awareness, and donor diplomacy.
- Hiring for Learning Agility: Employers prioritize adaptable mindsets and communicative confidence over static technical credentials.
- Cross-Institutional Mobility: Librarians benefit by rotating across different library sectors every 5 to 8 years to cross-pollinate skills.
