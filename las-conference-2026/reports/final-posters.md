## Raffles Girls' School: The Love Our Library Campaign
Hanis Idayu presented Raffles Girls' School (Secondary) Library's multi-year journey transitioning from traditional enforcement toward a participatory, student-shaped learning ecosystem. In shared academic spaces, common operational challenges—such as peak-period seat hoarding, uncontrolled acoustic spillover, and food litter—have historically been managed through punitive disciplinary notices and staff policing.

Partnering with the school's Student Leadership Board, RGS deployed a four-stage design methodology: observing everyday usage, identifying systemic friction, co-designing practical interventions, and conducting iterative reviews. The library implemented an RFID- and OPAC-integrated hybrid seating reservation system that balanced predictable study slots with drop-in flexibility, while acoustic observations were translated into live visual noise heat maps that guide students to appropriate study zones.

### RGS Library: four-stage student co-governance architecture
- 1. Ground Sensing — Usage audits & student surveys
- 2. Visible Indicators — Acoustic heat maps & RFID seats
- 3. Communal Care — Love quotient & peer cleanup
- 4. Co-Creation — Student-led spatial redesign

### Building Accountability Through Visible Community Metrics
To resolve housekeeping issues without shaming individuals, RGS introduced digital "love quotient" indicators and cleanup photo logs, allowing students to recognize the collective impact of individual habits. Furthermore, a student design committee directly planned and refurbished the library's recreational unwind zone.

The initiative earned an MOE Energy Commendation Award, marking an evolutionary progression from 2024's reactive reminders to 2026's self-regulating, student-governed ecosystem built around five pillars: fair access, responsive services, user autonomy, community ownership, and continuous learning.

## NLB: Scaling Heritage Storytelling via Curiosity
Lim Ting Soon (Ling Timson) detailed how NLB's digital history initiative Curiosity grew from an internal experiment in 2018 into a sustained digital heritage repository over seven years. While numerous institutional hackathon pilots fade quickly due to budget depletion or vendor dependency, Curiosity survived and scaled by maintaining a lean, self-reliant operational core.

Managed by an agile team of just 1 to 2 librarians without outsourced agency contracts, the platform transforms static digitised maps, photographs, and archival records into three interactive storytelling formats: walking heritage story maps, photo essays, and building timelines. Integrating generative AI models has halved initial drafting and asset aggregation cycles, with librarians remaining essential gatekeepers who fact-check narratives and preserve historical accuracy.

## NYP: Dual-User Architecture & Portal Rationalization
Faiza Ibrahimsa shared Nanyang Polytechnic Library's comprehensive overhaul during their mandated digital migration from legacy Adobe Experience Manager (AEM) infrastructure to the Singapore Government's standardized Isomer web platform. Rather than merely cloning existing web content, NYP utilized the milestone to audit systemic UX failures.

### The Dual-User Research Methodology
NYP recognized that portal health depends on two distinct user groups: Front-End Users (students and faculty requiring rapid resource discovery without excessive scrolling) and Back-End Users (librarians and content administrators hindered by duplication, complex maintenance, and IT dependencies).

Applying NYP's proprietary SPARK innovation model alongside the Double Diamond methodology, the team conducted task-based card sorting and co-design workshops. The migration pruned total page count by 53%, eliminated deep navigation trees, established sustainable internal content ownership, and raised overall usability ratings to 4.0 out of 5.0.

## Key Takeaways & Core Strategic Insights
### Executive takeaways
- Student Co-Management: Shared library facilities thrive when behavioral guidelines are co-created with students rather than policed by staff.
- Sustainable Lean Operations: NLB's Curiosity proves high-impact digital heritage platforms can thrive for years with small, agile teams.
- Human-in-the-Loop AI Acceleration: Generative AI halves digital content aggregation cycles, while librarians remain essential fact-checkers.
- Radical Portal Pruning: Eliminating 53% of legacy web sprawl significantly accelerates user discovery and reduces admin maintenance.

### Core strategic insights
- Environmental Nudges: Visual acoustic heat maps provide transparent information, empowering patrons to make informed spatial choices.
- Modular Living Assets: Structuring digital heritage into reconfigurable units enables cross-agency collaboration and educational reuse.
- Dual-User Design Principle: Digital library tools fail if back-end administrative workflows are ignored during front-end consumer redesigns.
- In-House Competence: Building solutions internally shields institutions from vendor lock-in and post-pilot abandonment.
