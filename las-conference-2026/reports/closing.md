## Executive Summary & Concluding Atmosphere
The 2026 Library Association of Singapore (LAS) Conference concluded with an energetic and celebratory closing address delivered by Conference Chair and LAS Vice President Ian Yap. Across two intensive days, the conference drew unprecedented engagement, doubling attendance from prior years as more than 400 delegates gathered within the University Cultural Centre to interrogate the future of libraries, technology, and librarianship.

Yap celebrated the community's resilience and high-spirited dialogue, noting that while external challenges—symbolized by the haze outside—threatened to overwhelm, the conference served as a protected incubator of collective purpose. Drawing together the defining conceptual frameworks forged across the two days, Yap shifted the professional narrative from passive anxiety to proactive stewardship: urging librarians to transition from being mere "humans in the loop" to asserting themselves decisively as "humans in the lead."

### 1. Human in the Lead
Rejecting passive oversight to assert human ethical leadership, purposeful direction, and agency over automated technologies.

### 2. Growing Organism
Invoking Ranganathan's Fifth Law: as information volume grows exponentially, library roles must dynamically evolve.

### 3. CONSAL 2028 Vision
Singapore confirmed as host for the Congress of Southeast Asian Librarians 2028, uniting over 1,000 regional leaders.

## Synthesizing the Conference Lexicon: Concepts That Endure
Reflecting upon the rich vocabulary generated across keynotes, panels, and paper presentations, Ian Yap highlighted how the 2026 conference succeeded in forging an elegant, durable conceptual lexicon for 21st-century information practice. Keynotes and discussions moved beyond sterile corporate jargon, establishing human-centered reference points that articulate the true societal value of memory institutions.

Yap highlighted core anchors: Warren Fernandez's warning against settled prejudices and call for the bi-literate brain; Assoc. Prof. Loh Chin Ee's research on reading as respite; Ng Kah Gay's framing of libraries as slow-down microclimates; and Sarah Ichioka's advocacy for public luxuries that shelter communities against economic and climate disruption. Above all, Yap emphasized the conceptual breakthrough coined during Day 2: replacing the passive engineering concept of "human-in-the-loop" with "Human in the Lead."

## Ranganathan's Mandate in an Age of Exponential Growth
Yap grounded his concluding synthesis in S.R. Ranganathan's timeless Fifth Law of Library Science: "A library is a growing organism." In 2026, growth is defined not by linear shelf acquisition, but by the exponential explosion of synthetic data, algorithmic feeds, and fragmented content.

In this hyper-saturated environment, Yap posed three core questions to every practitioner: When content aggressively competes for attention, who will champion deep, sustained reading? When data volume overwhelms citizens, who will serve as the expert navigator? And when content provenance becomes impossible to trust, who will stand as the verifiable guide? Technology alone will not dictate the future; information professionals must actively exercise agency to build those conditions.

## Celebration of Practice: Poster Presentation Awards
Prior to the formal closing address, the conference celebrated excellence in grassroots applied research, presenting awards for the top three poster showcases voted by conference delegates and evaluation panels. The awards recognized innovative operational interventions directly addressing the conference themes.

The top honours spanned academic workflows, digital storytelling, and data-informed librarianship: First Prize was awarded to Jessie Tan (National Institute of Education Library) for pioneering automated repository workflows using local open models without IT coding backgrounds; Second Prize went to Lim Ting Soon (National Library Board) for scaling the Curiosity heritage platform over seven years; and Third Prize was awarded to Muhammad Kurniawan (National Library Board) for embedding collection data analytics into Malay Library Services.

## Strategic Announcements: The Road to CONSAL 2028
Turning toward the future of the professional community, Yap unveiled three major association announcements for the Library Association of Singapore as it celebrates its 71st anniversary.

### Singapore Confirmed as Host for CONSAL 2028
Singapore will officially host the Congress of Southeast Asian Librarians (CONSAL) in 2028, welcoming over 1,000 regional delegates. In conjunction with this milestone, LAS will publish a landmark coffee table book showcasing Singapore's public, academic, and school libraries, accompanied by upcoming calls for volunteer planning committees.

Yap concluded by thanking the volunteer organising committee led by Vice Chair Benita, Chelsea, and Lindy, who delivered the two-day conference in nine months, alongside past LAS presidents in attendance (Mr. Ch'ng, Judy, Sally Jane), reaffirming the power of cross-generational volunteer dedication.

## Key Takeaways & Core Strategic Insights
### Executive takeaways
- Human in the Lead: Elevate the profession from passive tool consumers (in-the-loop) to ethical, strategic drivers (in-the-lead).
- Ranganathan in the AI Age: Exponential growth requires libraries to adapt dynamically while anchoring sustained reading habits.
- Grassroots Applied Excellence: Poster winners demonstrated how frontline staff innovate through local models, data, and storytelling.
- CONSAL 2028 Milestone: Singapore positions itself as the regional epicenter for Southeast Asian library collaboration.

### Core strategic insights
- Agency Over Technology: The future is not an inevitable technological force; it is shaped by what professionals choose to build.
- The Enduring Guild: Over 71 years, LAS's strength stems from selfless volunteerism and cross-institutional solidarity.
- Combating Information Anemia: As synthetic content proliferates, the library's value as a trusted provenance anchor increases.
- Community as Respite: Physical gatherings renew professional confidence, breaking isolation through collective problem-solving.
