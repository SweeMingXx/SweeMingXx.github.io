## Executive Summary & Thematic Overview
Serving as the capstone panel for Theme 3 and the conference proceedings, the session convened thought leaders across public, academic, polytechnic, and governmental information domains. Moderated by Alex Foo, the panel addressed the existential and operational dilemmas confronting the profession: agentic workflow integration, the shifting needs of AI-empowered users, professional accreditation debates, and the fundamental identity of librarianship.

The panel directly tackled controversial territory: Aaron Tay challenged librarians to advance beyond traditional Boolean logic toward information retrieval theory, agentic harnesses, and rigorous comparative auditing; Koh Chern Chern showcased how polytechnic libraries build competitive value by providing verified, equitable databases that outshine hallucination-prone web bots; and Gee Miaw Miin articulated a historic shift from qualification-based barriers toward competency-based pathways (LAS PDS revamp and NLB's dual-track specialist ladders), reaffirming the library's timeless mission to bridge societal divides between the information haves and have-nots.

## Core Skills: Agentic Workflows & Information Retrieval Theory
Synthesizing national workforce frameworks, Gee Miaw Miin opened by outlining five non-negotiable competencies: digital fluency, critical thinking, learning agility, creative problem-solving, and human-centered collaboration. She observed that librarians have demonstrated historical adaptability—transitioning seamlessly from physical catalog cards to digital systems, and now to generative workflows.

Aaron Tay pushed for a deeper technical benchmark. Moving beyond prompt engineering, Tay argued that librarians must master agentic harnesses—learning how code models orchestrate scripts, context windows, and autonomous sub-agents to solve unstructured problems. Crucially, Tay challenged the profession's search dogma: while librarians excel at Boolean query formulations, modern AI engines rely on semantic vector search and query expansion. To negotiate with vendors and advise researchers authoritatively, information professionals must master the underlying mathematics of information retrieval theory.

## Disintermediation & The Reality of AI-Empowered Users
Alex Foo provoked the panel with a stark scenario from digital journalism: as personal AI agents become ubiquitous, web portals cease to be destinations, reducing content providers to disintermediated raw data feeds. In response, Tay noted that while users previously consulted librarians after failed Google searches, they now seek assistance after ChatGPT fails to provide primary-source full text or verified subscription access.

Koh Chern Chern detailed how Singapore Polytechnic proves value by benchmarking proprietary databases against open web queries. Demonstrating that syllabi grounded in licensed publisher texts exhibit superior academic depth compared to open web crawls, Koh stressed that libraries provide vital equity: ensuring all students have verified access regardless of their ability to afford premium AI subscriptions.

## The Great Credential Debate: Is the MLIS Obsolete?
In one of the conference's most animated segments, Foo asked whether the traditional Master of Science in Library and Information Science (MLIS) remains necessary. A live room poll revealed zero hands affirming the MLIS as an essential prerequisite to enter the profession.

Gee Miaw Miin asserted that national and global hiring is transitioning decisively toward competency-based recognition. LAS is actively overhauling its Professional Development Scheme (PDS) to certify practitioners based on proven portfolio achievements rather than academic papers. In parallel, NLB established a dual-career track enabling specialist librarians to attain senior principal rank (equivalent to a civil service director level) based purely on domain expertise. Tay and Koh concurred that while foundational library principles matter, employers prioritize learning agility and the demonstrable capability to build domain-specific AI workflows.

## Can a Librarian Be AI-Resistant? (The Umberto Eco Ideal)
Invoking Umberto Eco's homage to the joy librarians experience when asked for help, Foo concluded by asking if a space remains for an analog, AI-resistant librarian. Gee responded with an emphatic analogy: resisting AI today is equivalent to demanding a career without email or word processors.

### Treating AI as an Intern, Not as a Boss or Teacher (Gee Miaw Miin)
"Look at AI as if it's an intern. You give them instructions, they do things to help you, but you need to check their work. You cannot treat them as your boss or your teacher, where you just take whatever they say as the gospel truth." Librarians must retain primary agency, professional skepticism, and ethical accountability for all final outputs.

Tay affirmed that the execution gap is closing: ideas once hindered by technical limitations can now be coded rapidly using AI assistants, freeing librarians to focus on complex, high-value user empathy.

## Key Takeaways & Core Strategic Insights
### Executive takeaways
- Post-Boolean IR Theory: Librarians must understand semantic vector search and agentic harnesses to audit modern black-box discovery.
- The Competency Pivot: The MLIS is no longer a rigid gatekeeper; LAS PDS and NLB dual-tracks establish portfolio-based advancement.
- Closing the Execution Gap: Generative coding tools allow non-technical librarians to rapidly build custom discovery solutions.
- The Timeless Equalizer: The core mission of the library remains bridging the gap between information haves and have-nots.

### Core strategic insights
- Grounded vs. Open Retrieval: Subscribed, publisher-curated collections provide verifiable grounding, mitigating generative hallucinations.
- Managing the AI Intern: Treat AI as an assistant to delegate routine work to, maintaining strict human editorial oversight.
- Redefining Professional Core: Stop obsessing over guild definitions; value is determined by community relevance and trust.
- Frontline Mobility: Enable continuous staff upskilling (e.g. shelvers mastering copy-cataloging) to build institutional agility.
