## Executive Summary & Strategic Context
The 2026 Library Association of Singapore (LAS) Conference opened with a decisive challenge to the library profession: in an era of exponential technological acceleration, how do memory institutions and knowledge workers maintain human agency and cultivate deep societal discernment?

LAS President Shameem Nilofar anchored the morning by reframing technological discourse: while AI is frequently portrayed as an unstoppable grammatical subject that disrupts and replaces, adaptation is an active human verb. Opening Keynote speaker Warren Fernandez addressed the existential anxiety surrounding generative tools. Drawing upon Harold Bloom's classic question—"Information is endlessly available to us, but where shall wisdom be found?"—Fernandez articulated three foundational pillars for the future of libraries: cultivating sustained readers, acting as guides to critical judgment, and curating living collective memory.

### 1. Human Agency
Librarians must act as the deliberate grammatical subjects of change, rejecting passive narratives of algorithmic displacement.

### 2. The Bi-Literate Brain
Balancing rapid information retrieval with deep, contemplative reading (festina lente) to preserve neural comprehension circuits.

### 3. Living Memory
Connecting institutional archival records with intimate community testimonies through compelling, interactive public encounters.

## Welcome Remarks: Reclaiming the Grammatical Subject
Opening the conference, LAS President Shameem Nilofar noted that the future envisioned during the 2012 Libraries for Tomorrow conference had arrived ahead of schedule. Exponential technological shifts often appear flat for years before suddenly becoming ubiquitous. Drawing upon the philosophy of Dr. Fei-Fei Li (Stanford Human-Centered AI Institute), Nilofar warned against linguistic framing that makes AI the subject and humans the passive object—"AI disrupts, AI replaces, AI decides."

Because adapting is an active verb, the responsibility of directing change belongs to librarians collectively. Marking LAS's 70th anniversary, Nilofar highlighted that while information tools evolve continuously, the association's mission remains unchanged: preventing individual isolation and professional burnout by providing a communal platform where academic, public, school, and special libraries can share parallel experiments and early failures openly.

## Pillar I: Cultivating Readers & the Bi-Literate Brain
Warren Fernandez refuted predictions of professional obsolescence, asserting that libraries become more essential as generative technologies proliferate. Citing neuroscientist Maryanne Wolf's Reader, Come Home, he emphasized that reading is an unnatural biological circuit that weakens without deliberate exercise. Amid declining PISA reading scores in Singapore (falling from 543 in 2022 to 535 in 2025) and globally, societies face acute attention fragmentation.

Fernandez advocated cultivating a bi-literate brain capable of both rapid information scanning and slow, contemplative deep-reading (festina lente—hurry slowly). Through the national Read SG movement, patronized by President Tharman Shanmugaratnam, libraries must build reading habits through gentle companionship, accessible entry points (graphic novels, detective fiction), and behavioral nudges, avoiding moralizing prescriptions.

## Pillar II: Guides to Critical Thinking & Knowledge Provenance
As automated summaries make stopping at the first plausible answer tempting, Fernandez invoked Confucius: "Learning without thought is labor lost; thought without learning is perilous." While AI generates coherent outputs in seconds, it conceals the methodological journey from primary source to generated text.

Librarians must serve as intellectual guides who lead patrons through deconstructive citation audits—interrogating source authority, opening referenced manuscripts, and revealing what nuances or alternative perspectives were erased during computational compression.

| Core Dimension | Algorithmic / AI Generation | The Guided Library Encounter |
|---|---|---|
| Source Lineage | Obscured behind statistical language models. | Transparent, traceable citations audited to origins. |
| Cognitive Mode | Instant summary; cognitive offloading (TL;DR). | Bi-literate synthesis; disciplined contemplative focus. |
| Information Literacy | Reflexive cynicism or uncritical acceptance. | Earned trust built on methodology and open verification. |

## Pillar III: Curators of Collective Memory & Interactive Encounters
Tracing the lineage of libraries from ancient Nalanda and Han China to the British Library and Singapore's 1837 origins at Stamford Road, Fernandez demonstrated that collections have always integrated artifacts, maps, and texts to investigate societal reality.

### The 1920s Toa Payoh Survey Map: Transforming Archives into Personal Inquiry
Recalling a conservator at the National Archives preserving a 1920s survey map of Toa Payoh, Fernandez noted how an archival artifact sparks personal inquiry: "Who lived here before flat complexes rose? Which traces remain beneath the familiar roads of our lives?" With exhibitions like the Albatross Files drawing over 260,000 visitors, libraries must connect official records with living community testimony.

Memory institutions must engage emerging technologies—such as AR, VR, and interactive storytelling—to present history from the viewpoint of historical actors who did not yet know how events would unfold, making archives relevant to contemporary generations.

## Strategic Directives & Institutional Recommendations
- Deploy Deconstructive AI Reference Audits: Teach patrons how to cross-examine AI answers by retracing citations, verifying source veracity, and discovering original authors rather than accepting synthetic summaries.
- Adopt Behavioral Nudging for Deep Reading: Model reading campaigns after public health behavioral interventions (e.g., HPB), encouraging citizens to replace doomscrolling with daily contemplative reading through low-barrier entry points.
- Redefine Institutional Success Beyond Throughput: Supplement operational metrics (footfall, loans, downloads) with qualitative transformation indicators: cultivating regular readers, critical discernment, and civic trust.
- Invest in Staff Capacities for Expanded Missions: Allocate dedicated professional development time for librarians in digital curation, data advocacy, and interactive historical exhibition design.

## Synthesis & Professional Outlook
The opening addresses of LAS 2026 underscored that libraries are not passive casualties of technological disruption, but essential sanctuaries for human discernment. By combining compassionate reading companionship, critical research guidance, and interactive historical curation, information professionals ensure that technological abundance enriches societal wisdom rather than eroding it.
