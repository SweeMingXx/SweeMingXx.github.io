# Voice of the Fraternity: The Complete Conference Quotations
A Definitive Compendium of Spoken Ideas, Provocations, and Philosophical Declarations

OFFICIAL CONFERENCE ANTHOLOGY · LAS CONFERENCE 2026 · 27–28 OCTOBER 2026

This anthology brings together the verbatim quotations, pedagogical philosophies, and debates recorded across the two days of the 2026 Library Association of Singapore Conference. Grouped by session and speaker, these statements reflect a profession asserting agency, safeguarding human dignity, and charting the future of knowledge stewardship.

## Day 1: Welcome Address & Opening Keynote (27 October)
> "Most sentences make AI the subject and people the object: AI transforms, AI replaces, AI disrupts, AI decides. AI does things and things are done to us. But adapting is a verb. A verb means a subject. Somebody has to do the adapting, and that somebody is us."
> Shameem Nilofar · President, Library Association of Singapore · WELCOME ADDRESS

> "No library in Singapore, however well-resourced, can work out the future alone... Exponential change experienced alone is exhausting. So when I say the subject of that sentence is us, I mean us together."
> Shameem Nilofar · President, Library Association of Singapore · WELCOME ADDRESS

> "Information is endlessly available to us. Where shall wisdom be found?"
> Warren Fernandez · Deputy Chair, National Library Board · OPENING KEYNOTE (CITING HAROLD BLOOM)

> "A person who will not read has no advantage over one who cannot... Reading is not an innate skill, unlike speech or sight. We humans have to learn to read. We either use it or we will lose it."
> Warren Fernandez · Deputy Chair, National Library Board · OPENING KEYNOTE (CITING MARK TWAIN & MARYANNE WOLF)

> "Over 2,000 years ago, Confucius expressed an important principle: Learning without thought is labor lost; thought without learning is perilous. I sometimes wish someone, some graffiti artist, would emblazon this on the walls of the White House."
> Warren Fernandez · Deputy Chair, National Library Board · OPENING KEYNOTE

> "Critical thinking requires more than just skepticism, or worse, cynicism or reflexive disbelief. If people leave an information literacy session convinced that nothing can be trusted, we have simply given them a different problem. People need to learn how trust can be earned through evidence, transparency, careful methods, and a willingness to correct mistakes."
> Warren Fernandez · Deputy Chair, National Library Board · OPENING KEYNOTE

## Theme 1: Future of Libraries Panel Discussion (27 October)
> "When we ask teenagers why they read, some actually say: 'I read to get away from the phone. I read because I need some quiet time by myself. I read to escape.' Reading is a place of respite."
> Assoc. Prof. Loh Chin Ee · National Institute of Education, NTU · FUTURE OF LIBRARIES PANEL

> "Naomi Baron makes me think when I talk to her. But when I use AI, AI gives me answers. And that's the equivalent of a good book—a good book or a good article should make us think."
> Assoc. Prof. Loh Chin Ee · National Institute of Education, NTU · FUTURE OF LIBRARIES PANEL

> "That's the problem with librarians: you do such good work and you seek to be invisible when you do it... When I ask my librarian, my librarian tells me how to think through the information. Librarians need to be a little bit less invisible."
> Assoc. Prof. Loh Chin Ee · National Institute of Education, NTU · FUTURE OF LIBRARIES PANEL

> "Actually, I feel that we can call our industries slow-down industries. Everything around us is like, let's get faster, let's get better in a faster way. But what is a slowing down that would create the conditions available for deep reading? We have to create microclimates to encourage deep reading."
> Ng Kah Gay · Publisher, Ethos Books & Pagesetters · FUTURE OF LIBRARIES PANEL

> "We must have confidence in human frailty—choosing the wrong book and learning from it, because that's how humans learn. If we go for outcomes, predictability and accuracy will be AI 100%. Is that how we're going to make a choice?"
> Ng Kah Gay · Publisher, Ethos Books & Pagesetters · FUTURE OF LIBRARIES PANEL

> "We need to create a culture of investing in public luxuries as a way of taking a stance against growing inequality: private sufficiency provided that we have these shared spaces and assets where we can all come and feel at home."
> Sarah Ichioka · Founding Director, Desire Lines · FUTURE OF LIBRARIES PANEL (CITING GEORGE MONBIOT)

> "Commercial AI models are designed to be sycophantic because their programmers know that's what keeps most of us coming back. But you can program for challenge... asking it to push back on your responses rather than giving you distilled summaries."
> Sarah Ichioka · Founding Director, Desire Lines · FUTURE OF LIBRARIES PANEL

## Day 1 Keynote Lecture: Language Diversity (27 October)
> "How can libraries utilize AI and yet remain focused and true to their mission? My answer to that is: communities, and not commodities. We have been a little bit silly by outsourcing all the good portions to elsewhere. We are missing the point by shipping out the development phase."
> Asst. Prof. Setsuko Yokoyama · SUTD · KEYNOTE LECTURE

> "Teaching computers how to speak indigenous language is more than just speech science. It's about connecting people with identity, place, and land... Nobody knows us as good as we know ourselves. Nobody knows our language as good as we know ourselves."
> Peter-Lucas Jones · CEO, Te Hiku Media · CITED BY ASST. PROF. YOKOYAMA

## Theme 2: Future of Technology Panel Discussion (28 October)
> "AI doesn't take away jobs, it takes away tasks. If you have a large chunk of your portfolio that can be automated, your job is high compressibility. But if it requires judgment and human dexterity, that is low compressibility. That's why dentists have a bright future."
> Lim Bee Kwan · CEO, Assurity Trusted Solutions; President, SCS · FUTURE OF TECHNOLOGY PANEL

> "Ten years ago, Geoffrey Hinton made a comment that we should stop training radiologists because technology would take over. Fast forward to today: the demand for scans went up so much because of lower costs that we actually need more radiologists. That is job elasticity."
> Lim Bee Kwan · CEO, Assurity Trusted Solutions; President, SCS · FUTURE OF TECHNOLOGY PANEL

> "AI lives in a box. It is basically existing knowledge retrieved faster. If you look back in human history, any breakthrough occurred in serendipity or was sparked by human inspiration. I don't think it is possible to qualify inspiration and serendipitous discovery within the AI box."
> Lim Bee Kwan · CEO, Assurity Trusted Solutions; President, SCS · FUTURE OF TECHNOLOGY PANEL

> "Who has the final say on culture? It's not Singapore. We can't be in charge of the culture for Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam, or Thailand. Indonesia alone has over 700 languages. Unless you've had a lived experience, it is really difficult to properly represent it digitally."
> Dr. Ngui Jian Gang (Chen Kang) · Head of Model Development, AI Singapore · FUTURE OF TECHNOLOGY PANEL

> "At the end of the day, the model is really just doing a best approximation of aggregated human knowledge. But aggregated also means that it's never going to be pushing at the edges. The ones doing that will be humans."
> Dr. Ngui Jian Gang (Chen Kang) · Head of Model Development, AI Singapore · FUTURE OF TECHNOLOGY PANEL

> "Right now with AI technology, we have so much information. The information is everywhere—it doesn't have value any longer. What has a value is meaning. And meaning is dynamic and generated through participation, through social activities."
> Dr. Natalia Grincheva · LASALLE College of the Arts, UAS · FUTURE OF TECHNOLOGY PANEL

> "On the neuroscience level, they proved that developing connections in neurons is painful. It is proved a physical pain that you experience. People who go through this develop knowledge inside their heart. When I see text generated by AI, I want to cry, because students don't have this knowledge base in their mind."
> Dr. Natalia Grincheva · LASALLE College of the Arts, UAS · FUTURE OF TECHNOLOGY PANEL

## Theme 3: Future of Librarianship Panel & Papers (28 October)
> "The value the library brings is to bridge the gap between the haves and the have-nots. In history, there will always be haves and have-nots—books in the 19th century, internet access in the 1990s, and now discernment in the age of AI. That underlying mission will never change."
> Gee Miaw Miin · Director, Resource Discovery & Management, NLB · FUTURE OF LIBRARIANSHIP PANEL

> "We should look at AI as if it's an intern. You give them instructions, they do things to help you, but you need to check their work. You cannot treat them as your boss or your teacher, where you just take whatever they say as the gospel truth."
> Gee Miaw Miin · Director, Resource Discovery & Management, NLB · FUTURE OF LIBRARIANSHIP PANEL

> "We are very expert in the understanding of Boolean, but a lot of AI search tools use semantic search and query expansion. We need to upscale in information retrieval theory so that we can test these tools and push back on vendors."
> Aaron Tay · Lead, Data & Research Information Services, SMU · FUTURE OF LIBRARIANSHIP PANEL

> "It used to be that finding out what users wanted was easy, but executing it was hard because libraries weren't technical. Now with AI, the execution gap is closing—you can code solutions rapidly."
> Aaron Tay · Lead, Data & Research Information Services, SMU · FUTURE OF LIBRARIANSHIP PANEL

> "Librarians spend too much time worrying about who we are as a librarian. It doesn't matter. As long as your community values you, they don't spend time debating guild boundaries."
> Aaron Tay · Lead, Data & Research Information Services, SMU · FUTURE OF LIBRARIANSHIP PANEL

> "In rare collections today, we have to be advocates and educators. We can no longer just say, 'Oh, I am just a librarian.' Moving forward, we must be master of some and jack of all trades."
> Makeswary Periasamy · Senior Librarian, Rare Collections, NLB · FUTURE OF LIBRARIANSHIP PAPER (THEATRE)

> "We can teach you the AI skills on the job. What we look for are quintessential librarian characteristics: learning agility, open-minded teamwork, and the ability to connect deeply with stakeholders."
> Caroline Pang · University Librarian, Nanyang Technological University · INTERVENTION DURING SMU JOB ADS DISCUSSION

## Official Closing Ceremony & Final Charge (28 October)
> "Human in the lead. Not human in the loop, which is what we always use. Human in the lead—that explains everything. That is what our role should be. And you heard it all here in 2026."
> Ian Yap · Conference Chair & Vice President, LAS · CLOSING REMARKS

> "Ranganathan reminded us almost a century ago that the library is a growing organism. If the library must continue to grow and evolve, then so must we. The future of libraries will not be shaped by technology alone. We have agency in shaping it. So let us leave this conference not merely asking what will the future bring, but what will I do to shape it?"
> Ian Yap · Conference Chair & Vice President, LAS · CLOSING ADDRESS
