## Executive Summary & Thematic Overview
Opening Day 2 under Theme 2 (Future of Technology), the panel convened senior leaders across government technology services, national AI model research, digital humanities, and library governance. Proudly sponsored by ACS Publications, the dialogue directly confronted the structural impacts of generative AI on professional labor, regional linguistic equity, and the emerging archival crisis surrounding interactive digital heritage.

The panel dismantled simplistic technological narratives: Lim Bee Kwan analyzed the changing design of work, explaining how AI compresses routine tasks while expanding demand through job elasticity; Dr. Ngui Jian Gang introduced AI Singapore's open-source SEA-LION ecosystem, cautioning that cultural representation cannot be monopolized by a single sovereign state; and Dr. Natalia Grincheva exposed the "flat-file blind spot" of legacy memory institutions, detailing how community-authored VR folklore currently lacks preservation infrastructure.

### 1. Workforce Compressibility
AI automates tasks rather than whole jobs, eroding entry-level rungs while elevating human verification and critical oversight.

### 2. Regional AI Commons
Open foundation models enable localized fine-tuning, safeguarding diverse Southeast Asian vernaculars against corporate bias.

### 3. Executable Preservation
Libraries must evolve from static file storage into discovery hubs capable of hosting executable code, sandboxes, and VR folklore.

## Workforce Dynamics: The Vanishing Bottom Rungs (Lim Bee Kwan)
Lim Bee Kwan addressed widespread anxiety regarding employment disruption, emphasizing that AI displaces tasks rather than entire professions. Applying the framework of job compressibility, roles composed largely of routine, codifiable duties face heavy automation, whereas functions requiring manual dexterity, nuanced empathy, and contextual judgment remain resilient.

To illustrate job elasticity, Lim cited Geoffrey Hinton's declaration a decade ago that radiology training should cease because algorithms would replace human diagnosticians. Instead, AI lowered the time and marginal cost per scan, causing clinical demand for diagnostics to surge and ultimately increasing total radiologist employment. However, AI automation creates an urgent dilemma: the vanishing bottom rungs of professional ladders. Because AI absorbs beginner drafting and basic coding, employers increasingly expect fresh graduates to operate with the maturity of mid-career specialists.

## Regional AI Infrastructure: Beyond Sovereign Monopolies (Dr. Ngui)
Dr. Ngui Jian Gang articulated AI Singapore's philosophy behind the open-source SEA-LION (Southeast Asian Languages in One Network) foundation model and the SEAL-eval evaluation suite. Rejecting the label of a "sovereign LLM," Dr. Ngui argued that Singapore cannot dictate cultural representation for neighboring nations—Indonesia alone encompasses over 700 spoken languages with vast regional idioms.

SEA-LION acts as an open, commercially permissive 70–80% baseline platform that regional partners adapt (e.g., Thai universities developing Yunchan Lion, or Indonesian enterprises fine-tuning logistics models). Strict copyright protocols exclude commercial books and scraped YouTube datasets, ensuring compliance across Hugging Face and cloud hyperscalers.

## The Archival Crisis: Preserving "VR Folklore" (Dr. Grincheva)
Dr. Natalia Grincheva presented a sharp critique of memory institutions, arguing that contemporary cultural production has moved beyond static physical media. For Singapore's 60th anniversary of independence (2025), her team co-designed an interactive virtual island circled by 60 virtual reality boats, each authored by multicultural citizens recounting oral migration narratives.

Despite winning an international Campus Award at the prestigious Ars Electronica festival, the project hit an institutional roadblock: state agencies and libraries had no infrastructure or metadata standards capable of ingesting or maintaining executable, immersive digital heritage. Grincheva identified this as the "flat-file blind spot"—mastery of digitizing static paper fails when confronted with interactive code.

| Heritage Dimension | Legacy Institutional Model | Executable Living Memory Model |
|---|---|---|
| Media Architecture | Flat files (digitized scans, PDFs, physical book catalogs). | Executable code, containerized software, interactive 3D/VR. |
| Epistemic Paradigm | Top-down institutional custody; knowledge as static storage. | Participatory discovery; dynamic, community-generated meaning. |
| Data Governance | Rigid state copyright and centralized institutional ownership. | Relational IP; community-held digital and cultural sovereignty. |

## The Neuroscience of Struggle & Human-Led Ideation
Addressing generative AI adoption, Grincheva cited neuroscience research showing that deep learning requires authentic cognitive friction: "Developing connections in neurons is painful—it is proved a physical discomfort." Bypassing this struggle through instant AI drafting produces homogeneous, emotionally hollow outputs.

### The Danger of Premature Ideation (Liana Tang)
Moderator Liana Tang warned that substituting human brainstorming workshops with instant chatbot queries eliminates interpersonal debate and shared group wisdom. While AI accelerates baseline tasks, meaningful consensus requires the productive tension of experienced colleagues debating in physical rooms.

Dr. Ngui emphasized that because foundation models merely statistically approximate aggregated past knowledge, they cannot independently explore intellectual boundaries—making human verification the defining skill of the future workforce.

## Key Takeaways & Core Strategic Insights
### Executive takeaways
- Tasks vs. Jobs: AI compresses routine tasks (high compressibility), leaving complex judgment and human care resilient.
- The "Vanishing Bottom Rungs": Removing beginner drafting widens the gap for graduates, requiring structured year-long internships.
- Decentralized AI Commons: Models like SEA-LION serve as open community baselines to prevent cultural centralization.
- Executable Preservation: Libraries must expand beyond flat PDFs to preserve interactive code, live sandboxes, and VR folklore.

### Core strategic insights
- From Storage to Discovery: With raw information commodified, library value migrates to meaning-making and verified provenance.
- The Friction of Learning: Neural connections require cognitive effort; uncritical AI offloading generates flat, generic work.
- Verification as Core Capital: The primary professional competency shifts from generation to auditing, guiding, and fact-checking.
- Federated Knowledge Networks: The future is not a monolithic universal library, but a network of self-sovereign community archives.
