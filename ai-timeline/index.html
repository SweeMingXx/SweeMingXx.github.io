Chapter 01 / The past

Narrow AI
Foundations.

1956 — 2016

From the Dartmouth Workshop to Deep Blue (1997) and AlphaGo (2016), AI proved it could excel within the boundaries of a task. These systems solved specific, highly constrained mathematical and perceptual problems—not general-purpose ones.

Dartmouth · 1956Deep Blue · 1997AlphaGo · 2016
A machine could win the game. It couldn't leave the board.

Chapter 02 / The breakthrough

The Generative
Revolution.

2017 — 2023

Google's 2017 Transformer paper helped unlock the scale era. GPT-3 (2020), Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, and ChatGPT (late 2022) brought a new kind of interface into everyday life: AI as an interactive creator of text, images, and media.

Transformers · 2017GPT-3 · 2020ChatGPT · 2022
The machine stopped just recognizing. It began creating.

Chapter 03 / The present

The Agentic
Shift.

2024 — Present

The shift moves beyond single-turn chat toward goal-driven, multi-step execution. Language models are paired with sandboxes, terminal access, APIs, and reasoning loops to plan, write code, use tools, and carry out workflows with greater autonomy.

PlanUse toolsIterateExecute
From “tell me how” to “work on it with me.”

Chapter 04 / The possible

Artificial General
Intelligence.

2027 — 2035 · Estimated

A proposed milestone: synthetic systems with cross-domain adaptability comparable to high-performing human cognitive capability. Forecasts vary considerably; 2027–2035 is a scenario window, not a promised arrival date.

Chapter 05 / The unknown

Artificial Super
Intelligence.

Post-AGI horizon · Theoretical

A theoretical phase in which systems surpass collective human intelligence. Its timeline—and even its path—depends on unknowns: whether self-improvement accelerates dramatically, or whether energy, compute, and physical constraints slow it down.
